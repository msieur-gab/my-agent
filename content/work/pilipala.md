## The situation

Children's days are rich but unrecorded. Adult reflection tools (journals, apps, trackers) impose categories that don't belong to children.

## What everyone assumed

A mood-tracking app: rate your day from 1 to 10.

## The reframe

Observability, not measurement. A child can say whether the sky was blue or grey. Asking them to score their mood teaches them to score, not to notice.

## What got built

A wooden peg board with eight rows of things a child can see or feel. Each day they place eight pegs. A phone camera reads the board in seconds and plays it back as an ambient soundscape made of real recordings. Boards accumulate on the wall, readable at a glance: the physical is the record.

## What changed

Screen time is measured in seconds: touch, read, hear, done. No accounts, no cloud, no tracking, and repairable with hardware-store materials.
