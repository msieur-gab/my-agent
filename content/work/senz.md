## The situation

Large corporations know they are too slow. Innovation labs exist because someone admitted it. At Allianz, the bottleneck was always the same: getting an idea from a sketch on a whiteboard to something people could see, test and react to. That needed developers, sprints and backlogs, machinery built to *build* things, not to *think about* them.

## What everyone assumed

That the answer was more speed: faster sprints, more developers, a better process.

## The reframe

The bottleneck was never code. It was the distance between an idea and something you could react to. The company's design system existed only as code, usable by developers alone. What if anyone with an idea could use it directly?

Two constraints came before any code:

- **Model honesty.** If the system can't recognise something, it says so instead of pretending.
- **Radical accessibility.** A pen, a piece of paper and a phone camera should be enough to start.

## What got built

You draw an interface on paper. Computer vision recognises the components, and Senz delivers a deployable MVP built on the company's own design system. No developer in the loop.

## What changed

Testing an idea became cheaper than the meeting about it. The speed wasn't the point, the agency was: people with ideas could test them without asking a pipeline for permission.
