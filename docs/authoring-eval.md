# The question bank, measured

26 October 2026, `authoring` branch. What changed, what was measured, what it means.
The raw tables are regenerated by `python3 tests/eval.py --sweep` into `tests/eval-results.md`; the `old` rows come from a one-off run of the previous matcher (match.js) on the same questions, before it was taken out of the home page.

## What changed

**Before.** Each piece carried four overlapping signals (`keywords`, `answers`, `questions/see`, `about`/`answer`), and `match.js` summed them with hand-tuned weights (3 per keyword phrase, 1.5 per shared word, 0.5 per field term, floors at 4.5 and 2). A wrong match gave no hint of which field to fix, and every edit moved every score. Whatever matched, the reply was the piece's one `about` line.

**After.** One file, `content/bank.md`: 90 entries, each a visitor's question, 2–5 other phrasings, the reply in my words, and where it leads (a piece, the list, the form, a page, or nothing). The visitor's sentence is compared with the 346 phrasings by meaning, with MiniLM-L6-v2 (int8, 23 MB) running in the browser; the vectors of the phrasings are computed at build time with the same model file (`build/embed.py`, onnxruntime). A word-overlap check runs first and answers instantly when a phrasing is nearly the same sentence. The pieces' `keywords` and `answers` no longer decide anything on the home page.

Three bands on the cosine: ≥ 0.56 the entry is the answer; 0.47–0.56 it is said as "the closest I have"; below, no answer, the form. When two entries within 0.04 of each other lead to different pieces, the page asks back with cards. When the matched phrasing isn't what was typed, the reply says how it read the question ("I read that as: …"), so a wrong reading is visible instead of silent.

## What was measured

Two question sets, none of their sentences in the bank:

- `tests/held-out.json`, written 1 October against the five texts, before the bank existed. 36 conceptual questions labelled with the piece that answers (20 factual ones, "how much does the chip cost", are left out: they ask for details inside the texts, which a question bank doesn't carry by design). 45 questions that must get no answer: near-misses about the projects, off-topic, requests for services.
- `tests/bank-eval.json`, written 26 October in another register than the bank (casual, long, jargon, typos). 67 questions labelled with the entry that answers, 31 that must get no answer.

Four matchers on the same questions: **old** (match.js on main), **words** (the bank by shared words only), **meaning** (the bank by MiniLM cosine), **page** (what the page does: words, then meaning).

### The floor

Cosine scores of the closest phrasing: answerable questions sit at a median of 0.66 (lowest 0.32), questions with no answer at a median of 0.34 (highest 0.95, see below). Moving the floor:

| floor | right | missed | false answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.41 | 93/103 | 4 | 17/76 |
| 0.47 | 91/103 | 7 | 8/76 |
| 0.50 | 87/103 | 13 | 6/76 |
| 0.56 | 74/103 | 27 | 4/76 |

0.47 keeps most of the answers and halves the false ones; between 0.47 and 0.56 the page says it's guessing.

## What it means

1. **Meaning beats words, by a lot.** Word overlap finds 13% of the rewordings; the embedding finds 87–91%. On the questions written before the bank existed, the bank goes from 50% to 83% right. The old matcher's remaining strength was keywords inside long texts, which the bank gives up on purpose.
2. **Authoring is the lever.** The first draft of the bank scored 68% on the rewordings. Reading the 20 misses and adding one or two phrasings to 20 entries (not the test sentences, other wordings in the same register) took a few minutes and brought it to 91%. That loop, read the no-answer log, add a phrasing, rebuild, is the whole maintenance of the agent now.
3. **MiniLM's weakness is still there.** It scores on wording more than on sense: "our corporate lab takes a year to ship anything" sits at 0.45 against "Our innovation lab is too slow", "is GPT-4 overkill for sorting invoices" at 0.32 against "Do we even need a large language model". The phrasings compensate, and the harness is ready for a better embedder (`build/embed.py` names the model once; e5-small or bge-small would re-measure with the same command).
4. **Some "false answers" are good replies.** "We need a new brand identity" now lands on "What do you not do?" (logos, marketing campaigns: no). The held-out labels from 1 October count that as a miss; I'd count it as the right reply. Four of the remaining ones are hedged with "this is the closest I have".
5. **The cost is the first download.** 23 MB model + 10 MB WASM runtime, served by the site, fetched the first time a question needs them and kept in the browser's cache after that. The three dots show while the model loads and reads. This is the thing to watch on a phone on the first visit.

## Bias

Both test sets and the bank were written by Claude, not by visitors: the rewordings are as different as one author can make them, which is less different than real people are. The numbers compare matchers fairly; they don't predict the hit rate on real questions. The no-answer form and the question log are what will.
