People want what you're building. They don't trust it.

They're right not to. Four in ten Europeans hold back from AI over their data, eight in ten want it kept in check, and most would pay more for a service they can rely on. That isn't a barrier to your product. It's the brief.

I put the human concern at the heart of the problem definition, design systems that cannot betray the people who use them, and build them to prove it.

Where this usually starts

An AI pilot that has to survive legal, and your customers.

It worked in the demo. Now it needs to pass the AI Act, the DPO and the person on the other side of the screen. Two-thirds of companies say security and risk, not technology, is what stops them scaling AI.3

A product that handles personal data and has to be trusted with it.

Compliance has an answer; your users want proof. The product can be the proof: a flow that never leaves the device is easier to explain than a policy.

A product for children, or families, that has to show it isn't taking anything.

The market is growing and the scrutiny with it. An object that works without an account, an app that needs fifteen seconds of screen: that's a business model, not a constraint.

Not on the list: legal advice, security audits, logos. I design how the product behaves and build it. Legal stays legal.

What changes for you

Legal says yes sooner, because there is something to review.

A product that proves its behaviour gets reviewed on what it does, not on a deck of promises. Dowgo went from first conversation to working code in the client's own stack in two weeks, and into production.

Trust becomes a line you can print on the box.

"Nothing leaves your device." "No account." "It says when it doesn't know." Claims you can make because the architecture makes them true, to the 58% who will pay more for exactly that.1

Fewer parts: less to pay for, less to defend, less to explain.

No large model where logic will do, no server where the device will do, no account where an object will do. Dowgo's document sorting runs in milliseconds for nothing; Pebbble has no database for anyone to breach or subpoena.

Your customers find out, without an ad.

A product people trust gets handed around. Vrooom's growth channel is a parent printing a badge; this page's is a question answered honestly.

Your team can carry it on without me.

Every decision written down with its reason, so the next feature and the next audit don't need me in the room.

Start with two weeks

A week of listening to how your people and your users actually work, to find the question behind the brief. Then a running proof of the answer, and the reasoning on paper. You end with something to show legal, the board and a customer, and you know whether to go on.
